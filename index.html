How Does Bangladeshi Media Cover the BNP? We Let AWS Find Out

A cloud-powered sentiment analysis of 30 political news articles across two outlets and two languages, built entirely on AWS serverless services.

🔗 View on GitHub
Quick Summary

What Is This Project About?

After Bangladesh's dramatic 2024 government resignation, political party BNP was all over the news. This project asks a simple but important question: do different news outlets report on BNP with different emotional tones? And does it matter if the article is written in Bangla or English? To find out, Katharina and Nahian scraped 30 articles from two major outlets, translated the Bangla ones into English using AWS, and ran sentiment analysis on all of them. The entire process ran on AWS cloud infrastructure with a total cost of under $1 per run.

What Does This Mean?

The Daily Star, which leans more secular and center-left, shows a slightly lower probability of neutral sentiment. This matches the finding that its two "mixed" articles use more emotional language overall. Prothom Alo's English articles lean slightly positive when they are not neutral, which fits with its conservative editorial stance that aligns more closely with BNP. Interestingly, Prothom Alo's Bangla articles show less positive lean than the English ones. This might mean the translation slightly neutralizes language, or it reflects a genuine difference in how the outlet writes for each audience.

Technical Approach

Three AWS Services, One Pipeline

🧱 Amazon S3

Stored raw and processed CSV files. Negligible cost. Enabled collaborative access between both researchers without sharing local files.

🌎 AWS Translate

Converted 10 Bangla articles into English. Handled in chunks due to the 5,000 character API limit. Biggest cost driver of the pipeline.

🧠 AWS Comprehend

Scored each article on four sentiment dimensions: Positive, Negative, Neutral, and Mixed. Used confidence scores, not just category labels.

Limitations

What This Study Cannot Tell Us

The results are real but narrow. Here is what limits how far we can take the conclusions:

  • ⚠ News articles are written neutrally by design. Professional journalism avoids emotional language, so sentiment analysis picks up very little signal. A topic-level or stance analysis would likely reveal more.
  • ⚠ 30 articles is a small sample. It may not capture the full range of each outlet's reporting patterns across different topics or time periods.
  • ⚠ Manual article selection can introduce bias. Even with care, the researchers who picked the URLs likely had some intuition about what "looked relevant," which could skew the sample.
  • ⚠ AWS Comprehend was built on Western text. It may miss political nuance, cultural framing, or indirect expressions of opinion common in South Asian journalism.
  • ⚠ Translation is lossy. AWS Translate does not always preserve political terminology, proper nouns, or tone shifts from Bangla to English.
Conclusion

What We Can Take Away

Bottom line

All three news sources reported on BNP in a remarkably similar tone. There is no strong evidence of editorial bias based on sentiment alone. The pipeline worked: AWS services were successfully combined to scrape, store, translate, and analyze political news across two languages at very low cost.

The bigger takeaway may be methodological. Sentiment analysis is a blunt instrument for political news, which is intentionally written to sound balanced. Future work should look at stance detection, entity-level framing, or topic modeling to find what sentiment misses.
